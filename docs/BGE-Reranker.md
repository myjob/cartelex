It is actually expected that the similarity sorting from pgvector and the ranking from FastRank (a reranker) differ significantly. While they both aim to find "relevant" content, they use fundamentally different architectures that look for different signals. [1, 2, 3]

# Why the rankings differ

The discrepancy occurs because of how each model "sees" your data:

**pgvector (Bi-Encoder):** Your Qwen embeddings are likely generated by a bi-encoder. This model converts your query and your document into fixed-length vectors independently. The similarity score (like cosine similarity) only measures how close these two "summaries" are in a multi-dimensional space. It is fast but "shallow" because it cannot compare the query words directly against the document words.

**FastRank (Cross-Encoder):** Rerankers like FastRank process the query and a document chunk together at the same time. This allows the model to perform "cross-attention," where every word in your query can interact with every word in the document chunk. This makes it much better at picking up nuances, such as negation or specific keyword importance, that a vector summary might lose. [2, 4, 5, 6, 7]

### Key Differences in Focus: pgvector vs. FastRank

| Feature | pgvector Similarity | FastRank Reranking |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Logic** | **Similarity:** "Do these two things look alike in general?" | **Relevance:** "Does this specific chunk answer this specific query?" |
| **Precision** | **Lower;** often pulls in "noisy" but semantically related results. | **Higher;** "slices through the noise" to find the most relevant answer. |
| **Computation** | **Very fast;** can search millions of rows in milliseconds. | **Slower;** too heavy to run on your whole database, so it only re-orders the "top-k". |

**Should they be similar?**

They should be correlated (a document with 0% similarity shouldn't suddenly become #1), but they will rarely be in the same exact order. In fact, if they were always the same, you wouldn't need a reranker at all. The entire purpose of using FastRank is to correct the "rough" ranking provided by pgvector and move the most truly relevant chunks to the top. [12, 13, 14, 15]
