The map shows both layouts on one keyboard. A highlighted key has the German (DE) character on top and the Swiss German (CH) character below, and identical keys are grey. The Overview view shows every layer at once, and Normal, Shift and AltGr isolate one layer each. In Overview, 20 of the 48 keys differ.

The letter keys are all identical, including the QWERTZ Y/Z swap. Nearly everything that differs is punctuation, umlauts and AltGr symbols:

- **Number row:** every key differs. In DE the shifted row is `! " § $`, while CH has `+ " * ç`.
- **Umlaut keys:** in CH, `ü`, `ö` and `ä` shift to `è`, `é` and `à` instead of capitals, so capital umlauts have to be typed with Caps Lock.
- **Right-hand keys:** DE has `+` and `#`, while CH has the dead key `¨` and `$`. CH has no `ß` and puts `'` where DE has `ß`.
- **AltGr symbols:** `@` moves from AltGr+Q (DE) to AltGr+2 (CH). In DE, `{ [ ] } \` sit on 7, 8, 9, 0 and ß. In CH, `[ ]` move to ü and ¨, `{ }` to ä and $, and `\` to AltGr+<.

I drew the Enter key as a plain wide key, and I left out a few rarely used AltGr characters such as `µ`.
