
The following information details the legal frameworks and categories of infringements regarding EU competition law.

---

Legal Framework: Article Enumerations by Treaty

The specific articles governing competition law have changed depending on the treaty in effect at the time.

| **Period**                | **Treaty**                         | **Horizontal / Licensing / Vertical / Joint Ventures** | **Abuse of Dominance** | **Member States** |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------- | ----------------- |
| **1.1.1958 – 30.4.1999**  | **EEC** (Treaty of Rome 1957)      | Article 85 EEC                                         | Article 86 EEC         | Article 90 EEC    |
| **1.5.1999 – 30.11.2009** | **TEC** (Treaty of Amsterdam 1997) | Article 81 TEC                                         | Article 82 TEC         | Article 86 TEC    |
| **1.12.2009 – Present**   | **TFEU** (Treaty of Lisbon 2007)   | Article 101 TFEU                                       | Article 102 TFEU       | Article 106 TFEU  |

> **Note:** The **TEU** (Treaty of Maastricht 1992) was established in 1993.

---


### Categories of Infringements

1. Horizontal Restraints (Article 101 TFEU)

Agreements between firms operating at the same market level (competitors).

- **Hardcore Cartels:** Includes price fixing, market sharing, joint sales agencies, and capacity restrictions.
    
- **Harmful Subcategories:**
    
    - 1.1 Price fixing
        
    - 1.2 Market sharing and quota cartels
        
    - 1.3 Bid rigging
        
    - 1.5 Export cartel
        
    - 1.9 Exchange of information
        
- **Subcategories generally not considered harmful by the EC:**
    
    - 1.4 Structural crisis cartel
        
    - 1.6 Trade associations
        
    - 1.7 Marketing and advertising agreements
        
    - 1.8 Agreements on standards
        

2. Abuse of Dominance (Article 102 TFEU)

Occurs when a single firm with significant market power prevents effective competition.

- **Subcategories:**
    
    - 2.1 Excessive pricing and discriminatory conditions
        
    - 2.2 Loyalty discounts and rebate schemes
        
    - 2.3 Predatory pricing
        
    - 2.4 Product bundling and tying
        
    - 2.5 Price squeezing
        
    - 2.6 Raising entry barriers
        
    - 2.7 Refusals to deal (essential facilities)
        

3. Licensing (Article 101 TFEU)

The transfer of rights to exploit Intellectual Property Rights (IPR). These can be anti-competitive if specific obligations are attached.

- **Subcategories:**
    
    - 3.1 Trademarks and branding deals
        
    - 3.2 Patents, know-how, and copyrights
        

4. Vertical Restraints (Article 101 TFEU)

Agreements between companies at different levels of the supply chain. They are harmful if they restrain trade, such as imposing maximum prices.

- **Subcategories:**
    
    - 4.1 Exclusive dealing and market foreclosure
        
    - 4.2 Territorial exclusivity and parallel import/export bans
        
    - 4.3 Resale price maintenance
        
    - 4.4 Bundling and tying
        
    - 4.5 Selective distribution systems (rarely)
        
    - 4.6 Franchising
        
    - 4.7 Discounts and rebates
        

5. Joint Ventures and Alliances (Article 101 TFEU & Article 2(4) EC Merger Reg)

- **Alliances:** Coordination of activities between companies.
    
- **Joint Ventures (JV):** Separate entities created for a specific purpose (e.g., new technology).
    
- **Subcategories:**
    
    - 5.1 R&D JV (mostly not considered harmful)
        
    - 5.2 Marketing, selling, distribution, and production JV
        
    - 5.3 Strategic and technological alliances
        

#### 6. Procedural & 7. Member States

- **Procedural:** Relates to Article 11 Regulation 17 (e.g., failure to supply information).
    
- **Member States (Article 106 TFEU):** Prohibition of Member States overruling EU competition law. Cases are brought specifically against EU member states.
